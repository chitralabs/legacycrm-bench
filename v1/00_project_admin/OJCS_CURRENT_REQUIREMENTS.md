## 1. Journal scope and aims

- OJ-CS is "a rigorously peer-reviewed forum for rapid publication (five weeks) of open access articles describing high-impact results in all areas of interest to the IEEE Computer Society." (Source: https://www.computer.org/digital-library/journals/oj/cfp-open-journal)
- The journal is "dedicated to publishing articles on the latest emerging topics and trends in all aspects of computing." (Source: https://www.computer.org/digital-library/journals/oj/cfp-open-journal)
- open.ieee.org describes the scope as encompassing all aspects of theory, design, practice, and application relating to computer and information processing science and technology. (Source: https://open.ieee.org/publishing-options/topical-journals/)
- **Fit assessment (my analysis, not an IEEE statement):** a reproducible LLM/benchmark/software-engineering paper falls within "all areas of interest to the IEEE Computer Society" and "all aspects of computing"; the journal's Call for Papers explicitly encourages linking datasets to IEEE DataPort "to support reproducibility," which aligns well with a benchmark paper. Scope fit is ultimately decided by the EiC at screening; pre-submission inquiry optional.

## 2. Manuscript types and page limits

- "The page limit for OJ-CS is 12 double-column pages." (Source: https://www.computer.org/digital-library/journals/oj/cfp-open-journal)
- The journal accepts peer-reviewed research articles describing high-impact results across IEEE Computer Society areas of interest; the Call for Papers does not enumerate additional manuscript types (letters/surveys/briefs). (Source: https://www.computer.org/digital-library/journals/oj/cfp-open-journal)
- Overlength charges: the 2026 IEEE Publications APC List shows OJCS as a fully OA title with APC $2,160 and, unlike hybrid titles (e.g., TC), lists no overlength page-allowance/fee columns for OJCS. (Source: https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/IEEE-Article-Processing-Charges-List.pdf — "2026 IEEE Publications APC List, Updated 6/11/2026")
- The Computer Society author-resources page indicates that its fully gold OA journals (including OJ-CS) do not assess overlength charges the way hybrid publications do. (Source: https://www.computer.org/publications/author-resources)
- Whether text beyond 12 pages is permitted at all (vs. hard limit) is NOT stated on the pages retrieved — see UNVERIFIED.

## 3. Abstract length

- OJ-CS pages retrieved do not state a journal-specific abstract limit; the IEEE Author Center (which OJ-CS directs authors to) states the abstract should "Consist of a single paragraph up to 250 words, with correct grammar and unambiguous terminology," be "self-contained; without abbreviations, footnotes, references, or mathematical equations," and include "3-5 keywords or phrases." (Source: https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/create-your-ieee-journal-article/create-the-text-of-your-article/structure-your-article/)
- Do "not include mathematical symbols in your article title or abstract because they may not display properly." (Source: same URL as above)

## 4. Reference style and reference-count guidance

- IEEE style governs; the official reference formatting document is the IEEE Reference Guide. (Source: https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/wp-content/uploads/IEEE-Reference-Guide.pdf)
- The IEEE Editorial Style Manual for Authors is the companion style document. (Source: https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/IEEE-Editorial-Style-Manual-for-Authors.pdf)
- No numeric reference-count limit was found on any official page. Guidance found: "Be sure to only cite references that directly support your work. Inflating citations by adding unnecessary references is considered a breach of publishing ethics." (Source: https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/create-your-ieee-journal-article/create-the-text-of-your-article/structure-your-article/)
- Tool: the "IEEE Reference Preparation Assistant" can verify reference lists before submission. (Source: https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/create-your-ieee-journal-article/authoring-tools-and-templates/tools-for-ieee-authors/)

## 5. Figure requirements

- OJ-CS Call for Papers: submit figures individually in PS, EPS, PDF, PNG, or TIF format; consult the IEEE Author Center for detailed specifications. (Source: https://www.computer.org/digital-library/journals/oj/cfp-open-journal)
- Resolution: "IEEE standards for black and white line art are >600dpi"; "IEEE standards for non-vector graphics for color and grayscale images are >300dpi"; vector formats (PS, EPS, PDF) are recommended. (Source: https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/create-your-ieee-journal-article/create-graphics-for-your-article/resolution-and-size/)
- Sizing: single column "3.5 inches, 88.9 millimeters, or 21 picas"; double column "7.16 inches, 182 millimeters, or 43 picas"; avoid sizing graphics smaller than one column wide. (Source: same URL as above)
- Accessibility: use both color and shape (not color alone) to accommodate color-vision deficiency. (Source: https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/create-your-ieee-journal-article/create-graphics-for-your-article/)

## 6. Supplementary material policy

- IEEE journals accept supplementary "multimedia objects such as video clips, source code, or data sets." (Source: https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/create-your-ieee-journal-article/prepare-supplementary-materials/)
- Accepted formats: text TXT/DOC/DOCX/PDF; images JPG/TIF/PNG/GIF/PDF/PS/EPS/BMP; video MP4/MOV/WMV/AVI (max 100 MB); audio MP3/AIFF/MOV/RA/WAV (recommended under 3 MB); datasets require a README with size stated. (Source: same URL as above)
- Supplementary materials are peer reviewed with the article; on acceptance, files plus README are compressed (.zip or .tar.gz) and hosted on IEEE Xplore with the article. (Source: same URL as above)

## 7. Data and code availability

- OJ-CS Call for Papers: authors are encouraged to upload related datasets to IEEE DataPort to support reproducibility and link them with published papers. (Source: https://www.computer.org/digital-library/journals/oj/cfp-open-journal)
- IEEE Author Center recommends authors "share associated data or code" in accessible repositories with DOI identifiers. (Source: https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/create-your-ieee-journal-article/authoring-tools-and-templates/tools-for-ieee-authors/)
- IEEE partners with Code Ocean, a cloud-based computational-reproducibility platform integrated with IEEE Xplore; code can be uploaded during review, at acceptance, or after publication, and receives a DOI. (Source: https://innovate.ieee.org/ieee-code-ocean/ — note this is an official ieee.org subdomain)
- No mandatory data/code deposit requirement for OJ-CS was found on official pages; DataPort/Code Ocean use is encouraged, not stated as required. (Sources: the two URLs immediately above)

## 8. IEEE policy on AI-generated text (as retrieved 2026-09-01)

- Policy text (IEEE Submission and Peer Review Policies, and IEEE Open guidelines): AI-generated content — "including but not limited to text, figures, images, and code" — "shall be disclosed in the acknowledgments section" of any article submitted to an IEEE publication. (Sources: https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/become-an-ieee-journal-author/publishing-ethics/guidelines-and-policies/submission-and-peer-review-policies/ and https://open.ieee.org/author-guidelines-for-artificial-intelligence-ai-generated-text/)
- The AI system used must be identified, and the specific AI-generated sections identified with a brief explanation of the level of AI use. (Source: https://open.ieee.org/author-guidelines-for-artificial-intelligence-ai-generated-text/)
- Exception: editing/grammar enhancement "is generally outside the intent of the above policy"; disclosure in that case is recommended, not required. (Source: https://open.ieee.org/author-guidelines-for-artificial-intelligence-ai-generated-text/)
- Reviewer-side restriction (context): manuscript content "shall not be processed through a public platform" for AI generation of review content. (Source: https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/become-an-ieee-journal-author/publishing-ethics/guidelines-and-policies/submission-and-peer-review-policies/)

## 9. Submission platform

- The OJ-CS Call for Papers directs submissions to the IEEE Author Portal at https://ieee.atyponrex.com/journal/oj-cs. (Source: https://www.computer.org/digital-library/journals/oj/cfp-open-journal)
- Context: the Computer Society author-resources page describes ScholarOne as the legacy platform and states the Society "is in the process of migrating its periodicals to the IEEE Author Portal submission platform." (Source: https://www.computer.org/publications/author-resources)
- Conclusion: for OJ-CS specifically, use the IEEE Author Portal link above (per the journal's own CFP page).

## 10. Article Processing Charge (APC)

- 2026 APC for OJCS (fully open access): **$2,160 USD**. (Source: https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/IEEE-Article-Processing-Charges-List.pdf — "2026 IEEE Publications APC List, Updated 6/11/2026"; PDF text extracted locally, row: "Computer Society, IEEE Open J. / OJCS / Full / $2,160")
- Discounts: "IEEE members will receive a 5% discount, and members of IEEE societies or councils will receive a 20% discount off OA article processing charges." (Source: https://www.computer.org/publications/author-resources)
- Waivers: "Corresponding authors from low-income countries are eligible for waived or reduced APCs"; waiver requests with documentation go to **pubs-waivers@computer.org**. (Source: https://www.computer.org/publications/author-resources)
- Institutional open-access agreements may cover pre-paid APCs, varying by institution. (Source: https://open.ieee.org/publishing-options/topical-journals/)

## 11. ORCID requirement

- "IEEE journals require an ORCID for all authors"; a registered ORCID is needed to submit for peer review and to review the article proof after acceptance; registration is free. (Source: https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/create-your-ieee-journal-article/authoring-tools-and-templates/tools-for-ieee-authors/)
- Submission checklist: "Get an Open Researcher and Contributor ID (ORCID) if you do not have one already." (Source: https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/submit-your-article-for-peer-review/checklist-for-submitting-your-article-for-peer-review/)
- Computer Society: ORCID "is required by all IEEE publications" and is prompted for at submission. (Source: https://www.computer.org/publications/author-resources)

## 12. Article template (Word and LaTeX)

- Format the article using the IEEE Open Journals article template, available in Word and LaTeX from the IEEE Author Center. (Source: https://www.computer.org/digital-library/journals/oj/cfp-open-journal)
- Templates are obtained via the interactive IEEE Template Selector: https://template-selector.ieee.org/ (Source: https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/create-your-ieee-journal-article/authoring-tools-and-templates/tools-for-ieee-authors/ieee-article-templates/)
- "Using an article template is required for journal submissions" at the IEEE Computer Society. (Source: https://www.computer.org/publications/author-resources)
- The exact LaTeX class/file name of the Open Journals template could NOT be verified from the retrieved pages (the Template Selector is an interactive tool) — see UNVERIFIED.
